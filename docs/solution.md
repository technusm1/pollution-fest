# Draft: feed Punjab's distilleries with Punjab's maize

Status: DRAFT, overnight 2026-11-12/13. Not on the live site. Sources are listed in `docs/research/*.md` (✓ = verified on the page). "Estimate" marks our own arithmetic.

## The one-paragraph version

Punjab already has a buyer for a better kharif crop. It's the grain-ethanol distilleries the state attracted: 18 plants that "need 35 lakh tonnes of maize a year" against ~5 lakh tonnes grown. Fed by Punjab kharif maize, that demand alone covers roughly 0.6–0.8 million hectares, the "1 million hectares off paddy" every expert committee since 2002 has asked for. Instead, the system feeds distilleries rice: grown with Punjab's groundwater, bought at MSP by FCI at an economic cost of ₹4,173/quintal, then sold to them at ₹2,320. In 2025-26 that rice-to-fuel route cost roughly ₹8,000 crore (estimate) and pushed Punjab maize below its MSP. Then, from November 2026, the oil companies cut the price they pay for maize ethanol (₹71.86 → ₹68.86/L) while the rice keeps flowing at ₹60.32/L. Make rice pay its true cost (or simply stop the discount) and the distilleries' own economics make local maize their feedstock. Let them sign multi-year, written contracts with farmers, PepsiCo-style. At hybrid yields and a price near MSP, kharif maize → wheat out-earns paddy → wheat, so farmers switch because it pays. Nobody's free power or MSP is touched. The straw problem leaves with the paddy: maize is harvested by early October, and its stalks are fodder.

## Is this new? No, and that's the point

The fix is already on the table, from every direction:
- **Economists:** ICRIER (Gulati & Adhikary, 2026): FCI rice "should remain a residual feedstock"; a hidden loss of ~₹10,000 crore per 5 Mt.
- **The government's own Food Secretary** (March 2026): "From next year, the government will stop supplying whole-grain rice from FCI stocks to distilleries."
- **Punjab civil society:** the Shiromani Punjab Forum (Oct 2026) proposed a "Punjab kharif maize compact" with assured buying and mandi dryers.
- **Farmer unions:** BKU (Mann), August 2026: "concessional FCI rice should not be allowed to undercut maize."

And yet, in October–November 2026, the maize ethanol price was cut, FCI rice for ethanol was capped at 3.24 billion litres for most of the year (still flowing), and its price was raised only to ₹2,390. **The question for the site isn't "why did nobody think of this?" but "everyone agrees; why is the opposite happening?"** This project can show the numbers that make it hard to ignore.

## The forest, in six facts

1. **Rice is a policy crop in Punjab, not a climate crop.** Rice area grew from 2.27 lakh ha (1960-61) to a record 32.46 lakh ha (2025) "due to its assured procurement at MSP". Normal rainfall is 565 mm; rice gets 160–180 cm of irrigation.
2. **Rice procurement explains at least half of the water-table decline** (Nature Communications 2024). 111 of 153 blocks are over-exploited; extraction is 156% of recharge (CGWB 2025).
3. **The water law made the burning worse.** The 2009 Subsoil Water Act delayed transplanting, squeezed the window before wheat, and raised peak fire intensity by 39% (Nature Sustainability 2019); fires +21% with a 10–12 day shift into November (ISRO-IIRS, Scientific Reports 2022).
4. **Every scheme pushed against paddy's advantages** (one-off cash, unbacked MSP promises, machines, fines): 5–25% of targets met; paddy area at a record anyway. (`research/tried-schemes.md`)
5. **Maize loses to paddy because of the buyer and the yield, not the crop.** CACP's Punjab data: paddy nets ~₹33,400/acre more, but maize's farm yield is 35–43 q/ha against hybrid potential of 52–62, and its MSP is not realised (Tarn Taran, June 2026: "not a single producer has received the MSP").
6. **The same state that pays to diversify pays more to undercut diversification.** Punjab's diversification budget: ₹500 crore planned, ₹0.85 crore spent (2024-25, ICRIER). The rice-to-fuel discount: ~₹8,000 crore in 2025-26 (estimate: (₹4,173 − ₹2,320)/q × 44.2 lakh t).

## The numbers that make it work

Break-even from CACP's Punjab cost data (TE2023-24): paddy net over A2+FL ₹91,830/ha (₹37,163/acre); maize A2+FL cost ₹59,193/ha. Wheat after maize yields 4–5 q/ha more (we use 1.8 q/acre at the 2027-28 wheat MSP of ₹2,610).

| Maize price (₹/q) | Break-even yield (q/acre) | (q/ha) |
|---|---|---|
| 2,200 | 25.6 | 63 |
| 2,410 (MSP 2026-27) | 23.4 | 58 |
| 2,600 | 21.7 | 54 |
| 2,800 | 20.2 | 50 |

- PAU's recommended hybrids: 21–25 q/acre (PMH 17: 25, "suitable for ethanol"). ICAR-IIMR demonstrations: up to 77 q/ha. Punjab's farm average: ~17.4 q/acre (43 q/ha).
- What a distillery can afford (estimate): ~₹2,550–2,950/q at the 2025-26 maize ethanol price, ~₹114/q less at the cut price. Distillers' own association said maize at ₹24/kg worked and ₹27–29/kg doesn't.
- **So the switch pays when a farmer gets hybrid seed, timely sowing and a buyer at about MSP, and a distillery can pay that price.** Contract farming supplies the first three, as PepsiCo did for potatoes in Punjab.
- Conservative: this ignores paddy's straw-management cost, maize's lower labour and pumping, and the silage option (₹24,000–28,000/acre paid for standing green maize in Bathinda, 2020–21).

Scale check: 35–37 lakh t of maize ÷ 4.4 t/ha (today) ≈ 0.8 Mha; ÷ 6 t/ha (hybrids) ≈ 0.6 Mha. Paddy covers 3.25 Mha. PAU's own estimate: Punjab needs ~6 lakh ha under maize.

Fiscal check (estimate): a ₹300/q price guarantee top-up on 3.3 Mt of Punjab maize costs ~₹1,000 crore a year, about an eighth of the 2025-26 rice-to-fuel discount.

## What has to change

1. **Price FCI rice for ethanol at its economic cost**, or follow through on the Food Secretary's March 2026 plan to stop whole-grain sales. Neutral, not anti-rice: distillers then pick the cheapest real feedstock, and local maize wins on freight.
2. **Restore the maize ethanol price** (₹71.86 → ₹68.86 was cut in Nov 2026) or tie feedstock prices to water use, as the Shiromani Punjab Forum proposed.
3. **Multi-year written contracts between distilleries and farmers/FPOs**: hybrid seed, field staff, a published grading spec and payment within days. Punjab's Contract Farming Act 2013 was repealed, so the rules need restoring. The PepsiCo record shows the failure mode: arbitrary quality rejections drive small farmers out.
4. **Dryers at the distillery gate.** Wet monsoon maize crashed to ₹700–1,300/q in mandis; distilleries can take it wet and dry it themselves.
5. **Kharif only.** Spring maize uses as much water as paddy or more (PAU). Contracts should specify late May–June sowing.

Who loses? No farmer's power or MSP. FCI stops losing money on fuel rice. Distillers pay more than for discounted rice but get a steady local supply. The rice surplus becomes visible (central stocks were ~3× the buffer norm in July 2026), which is the real conversation to have.

## Risks (honest)

- **Monsoon risk:** waterlogging, fall armyworm, wet grain. Bihar's maize boom is rabi (75 q/ha) not kharif (26 q/ha). Punjab hybrid trials are better (47–62 q/ha), but farm-level reliability needs proof.
- **Policy-made demand:** ethanol demand depends on OMC prices and allocations, as the Nov 2026 price cut shows. Multi-year contracts are what protect the farmer.
- **ICRIER's caution:** 43.8% of India's maize area is irrigated; a full well-to-tank comparison is needed. Kharif maize in Punjab needs 4–6 irrigations vs 20–25 for paddy, so the Punjab case is strong, but say so carefully.
- **Food vs fuel:** India exported ~2.5 Mt of corn in MY2025/26, so there is headroom today.
- **Not all of Punjab:** start with over-exploited blocks within a short haul of distilleries.
