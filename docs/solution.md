# Draft: feed Punjab's distilleries with Punjab's maize

Status: DRAFT, 2026-11-12/13 overnight. Not on the live site. Every figure links to `docs/research/*.md`, where each source is listed and marked verified (✓) or not. Items tagged **[CHECK]** are still being verified.

## The one-paragraph version

Punjab doesn't lack a buyer for a better crop. It built one: grain-ethanol distilleries that will need about 37 lakh tonnes of grain a year. That's enough kharif maize to take roughly 0.6–0.85 million hectares out of paddy, the "1 million hectares" that every expert committee since 2002 has asked for. Today the system feeds those distilleries rice: grown with Punjab's groundwater, bought at MSP by FCI, then sold to distillers about 40% below what it cost. The cheap rice undercut maize, and in 2026 maize sold well below its MSP. Stop selling below-cost rice for fuel and the distilleries' own economics make Punjab maize their natural feedstock (it's local, so freight is cheap). Let them contract directly with farmers, PepsiCo-style: hybrid seed, field staff, a fixed price and their own dryers. At hybrid yields and a price near MSP, maize then wheat out-earns paddy then wheat, so the farmer switches because it pays. Nobody's free power or MSP is touched. Straw burning ends where paddy ends: maize stalks are fodder, and the harvest is done by early October.

## Why this is "the sun, not the wind"

Every past scheme (see [tried-schemes](research/tried-schemes.md)) either pushed against paddy's advantages with one-off cash, or promised an MSP with no buyer behind it, or policed the farmer. All three are "the wind". This proposal changes no rule for the farmer. It removes a distortion upstream (below-cost rice going into fuel), so a private buyer with its own reasons to pay appears next to the farm. The farmer chooses.

## The forest, in five facts

1. **Rice is a policy crop in Punjab, not a climate crop.** Rice area grew from 2.27 lakh ha (1960-61) to a record 32.46 lakh ha (2025) "due to its assured procurement at MSP". Punjab's normal rainfall is 565 mm; rice needs about 160–180 cm of irrigation there. ([groundwater-history](research/groundwater-history.md))
2. **Rice procurement accounts for at least half of the water-table decline.** 111 of 153 blocks are over-exploited, and extraction runs at 156% of recharge. (Nature Communications 2024; CGWB 2025)
3. **The water law made the burning worse.** The 2009 Subsoil Water Act delayed transplanting, which squeezed the window before wheat; peak fire intensity rose 39% (Nature Sustainability 2019), and burning rose 21% with a 10–12 day shift into November (ISRO-IIRS, Scientific Reports 2022).
4. **Maize lost to paddy because of the buyer, not the crop.** By CACP's own Punjab data, paddy nets ~₹33,400/acre more. But the gap comes from farm yields of 35–43 q/ha against hybrid potential of 52–62, plus a price that is never realised. ([maize-economics](research/maize-economics.md))
5. **The state undercut its own diversification.** The ethanol boom made maize India's top ethanol grain (2023–25). Then FCI sold surplus rice to distillers ~40% below acquisition cost, maize crashed to ₹1,500–2,000/q against a ₹2,410 MSP, and Tarn Taran farmers said "not a single producer has received the MSP" (June 2026). Meanwhile the state's own diversification budget went from ₹500 crore planned to ₹0.85 crore spent.

## The numbers that make it work

PAU's recommended hybrids yield 21–25 q/acre (PMH 17: 25 q/acre, "suitable for ethanol"); ICAR-IIMR demos reach up to 77 q/ha. The farm average is 43 q/ha. **So the switch pays for a farmer who gets hybrid seed, timely sowing and a buyer at about MSP.** Those are exactly what a contracting distillery supplies.

This is conservative: it ignores paddy's straw-management cost, maize's lower pumping and labour needs, and the option to sell the standing crop as silage (₹24,000–28,000/acre paid in 2020–21 in Bathinda).

## What has to change (and who loses)

1. **Stop selling below-cost FCI rice for ethanol.** **[CHECK: FCI sale price vs economic cost, tonnes per year]** The loss on that rice is already public money; it's paying to keep the wrong crop in the ground.
2. **Let distilleries sign multi-year, written contracts with Punjab farmers or FPOs**, with a published grading spec and payment within days. PepsiCo's potato programme shows the model (seed, field staff, fixed price, on-time pay) and its failure mode (arbitrary quality rejections drive small farmers out). Punjab's Contract Farming Act 2013 was repealed, so the rules need to be clear.
3. **Dryers at the buyer's gate, not the farmer's.** Wet monsoon maize crashes to ₹700–1,300/q; a distillery has every reason to buy wet grain and dry it itself.
4. **Kharif only.** Spring maize uses as much water as paddy or more (PAU). The contracts should specify kharif sowing (late May–June).

Who loses? Nobody's power or MSP. FCI stops losing money on fuel rice. Distillers pay more per tonne of feedstock than for cheap FCI rice, but get a reliable local supply with low freight. **[CHECK whether distiller margins work at ~₹2,400–2,800/q maize]**

## Risks (honest)

- **Monsoon:** waterlogging and fall armyworm. Kharif maize in Bihar yields 26 q/ha against 75 in rabi, so kharif is harder. Punjab's 47–60 q/ha trial and hybrid figures are more relevant, but farm-level reliability needs proof.
- **Policy-made demand:** ethanol demand depends on OMC prices and blending targets. A contract farmer is exposed to the next policy U-turn, which is why multi-year contracts matter.
- **Rice stock pile-up:** if FCI can't dump rice into fuel, surplus stocks grow. That's the true cost of over-procurement made visible, and it's an argument for the shift, but expect pushback.
- **Food vs fuel:** using maize for fuel competes with feed. India became a corn exporter in MY2025/26 (~2.5 MMT), so there is headroom today.
- **Not all of Punjab:** maize suits some districts and soils better than others. The target is the worst-groundwater blocks near distilleries first.

## What this project can build to prove it
